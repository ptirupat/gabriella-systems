Batting Intelligence

What happened on this ball?

For batting clips, Gabriella tracks ball, bat, and body where visible, then returns metrics and visualizations suited to that camera view.

Four dimensions of every delivery

Ball, bat, body, and outcome — where the clip and view allow

Ball

  • Speed (quality-gated)
  • Trajectory (where visible)
  • Line and length (view-dependent)
  • Bounce point (view-dependent)

Bat

  • Bat path (where visible)
  • Bat speed at impact
  • Bat angle (where visible)
  • Contact time in this clip

Body

  • Head stability
  • Foot movement (pose-dependent)
  • Stride length (pose-dependent)
  • Joint positions

Outcome

  • Contact location (view-dependent)
  • Shot trajectory (where visible post-contact)
  • Bat-ball contact quality (quality-gated)
  • Session review (same-view clips only)

What's live, conditional, and research-stage

Capability status for batting analysis

Availability depends on camera view, calibration, visibility, and clip quality. The system reports "can't measure" when a metric cannot be reliably determined — it never substitutes a guess.

Coaching questions, not engineering metrics

Answers the questions coaches actually ask

When did contact happen in this take?

Contact timing in this take — measured in milliseconds from clip start to bat-ball contact. Useful as a same-view marker across sessions, not an early/late judgment.

Is my head staying still through the shot?

Pose overlays help coaches review head and upper-body stability through the shot — alongside bat and ball tracking where visible.

How consistent is my stride across the session?

Compare stride length and foot position across deliveries in the same session — where clips share a similar camera view and quality.

Session intelligence

Compare deliveries. Track progress.

Review metrics across clips when takes share a similar view and quality. Longitudinal trends are the goal — strongest with consistent capture.
