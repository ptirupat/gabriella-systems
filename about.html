About Gabriella Systems

Every athlete deserves structured evidence, not just impressions

Gabriella Systems builds computer vision infrastructure for bat-and-ball sports — making movement intelligence accessible, with honest estimates.

Who we are

Gabriella Systems builds computer vision infrastructure for cricket — and, in time, for baseball and softball. We combine computer vision and trustworthy AI so that coaches and athletes have objective performance evidence from ordinary practice video.

Mission

Make movement intelligence accessible and trustworthy for every cricket team, academy, and athlete

We build AI systems that turn ordinary practice video into structured, honest performance evidence — so coaches can see what changed, and athletes can understand how.

Vision

Clear about what we estimate, and what we can't yet

Gabriella shows coaches what our software picks up from ordinary net video, and is clear about what it can't estimate yet.

Why we exist

The problem we set out to solve

Most cricket coaching still relies on video as a memory aid, not as structured data.

Technical changes in batting and bowling are often small — a shoulder opening a fraction earlier, a knee angle shifting by a few degrees, a release timing changing by a handful of frames. These details are difficult to see consistently in real time and difficult to recall reliably from session to session.

Place the camera where the coach needs it, so Bat and Head come from a view that can see them, and get evidence that matches that view. Results follow the camera view you use. We do not invent a number the camera cannot see.

Accessible capture

Analysis should work from ordinary video at the nets — no specialist hardware required to start.

Honest metrics

Metrics follow the camera view. We do not invent a number when that view cannot support it.

Progress, not snapshots

Single-session impressions are not useful. Reproducible trends across sessions are.

Coaches stay in control

Our system surfaces evidence and supports coaching judgment — it does not replace it.

What we stand for

Our values

Evidence, not impressions

Coaches deserve structured data they can compare across sessions, not one-off visual impressions that are hard to recall or repeat.

Clear about each view

We say what a camera view can show. When the view cannot support a metric, we do not invent a number.

Human authority matters

AI should support coaches, not replace them. Our system surfaces evidence and priorities — the coaching decision remains with the coach.

Progress over ranking

The most useful comparison is an athlete against their own baseline, not against others. Individual progress is the goal, not league tables.

Research-grounded practice

Our approach is built on active research in computer vision, movement representation, and trustworthy AI — not assumptions.

Research

Product and research, together

Gabriella's product is built on active research — in visual AI, movement representation, uncertainty-aware estimates, and responsible AI for sport. Every improvement to how we find, track, and estimate feeds back into the system athletes and coaches use.

We also study harder questions — responsible AI for sport, clear estimates, and what good AI design looks like when the athlete is still developing.

Research

Four questions driving our research

Each pillar is an open set of research questions — not a finished product. Research publications are in development and will be announced following formal review.

Athlete Perception

Understanding athletes, equipment, and movement from ordinary video.

  • Human pose and movement understanding
  • Bat and ball tracking
  • Temporal action understanding
  • Key moments in the action
  • Occlusion and viewpoint robustness

Movement Intelligence

Moving beyond pose estimation toward understanding how movement unfolds through time.

  • Batting sequence and timing
  • Bowling phase analysis
  • Hip–shoulder coordination
  • Repetition consistency
  • Session-to-session change
  • Interpretable movement metrics

Trustworthy Sports AI

Sports AI should communicate when an estimate is available — and when it is not.

  • View-aware estimates
  • Analysis confidence
  • Uncertainty estimation
  • Metric reliability
  • Camera-angle sensitivity
  • Transparent limitations
  • Reproducible estimates

Youth Safety & Responsible AI

AI for young athletes should be designed differently.

Young athletes are not simply smaller versions of adult athletes. Age, developmental stage, coaching context, privacy, and how feedback is communicated can all affect whether AI is useful.

  • What safeguards should exist for video of minors?

Current work starts with cricket batting and bowling, and is intended to extend to baseball and softball. We welcome research conversations — use the research option on Connect.
